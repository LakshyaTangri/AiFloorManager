# M05 — Camera assessment, discovery and the local credential vault

**Module:** M05 device connectivity · **Subsystem:** S02 · **Controls:** C1, C8
**Implements:** F02 (assessment), F06 (adapters, discovery, credentials) · **Depends:** M01, M06
**Blocks:** M07 (decode), commissioning sign-off
**Class:** Platform (brief + code review; bench test required with real cameras)

## The score is a contract, so it is closed-set

F02 makes `counting_score` the basis of the accuracy commitment. A number that can be argued with
later is worthless, so `issues[]` is an enum (`Issue`) with no free-text path, and every issue names
a remedy the engineer can act on during the visit.

The score is a weighted quality sum with a multiplicative penalty per issue. The penalties are
severe on purpose: a stream carrying a named issue lands below the threshold, so the flag and the
number cannot disagree. A shallow, backlit camera scores in the low 0.2s — the shape SPEC's own
example shows — and a clean 1080p camera at a 35° depression scores 0.92.

`COUNTING_THRESHOLD` is 0.60 here. F02 R2 refers to a *published* threshold and SPEC publishes no
number, so this is a working value recorded as **ADR-REQ-010** and deliberately kept in code rather
than in the policy bundle: a threshold that shipped in signed policy could be lowered per site after
the contract was written.

## A bad stream must be resolved, not noticed

`commit()` refuses to produce an `AccuracyCommitment` while any below-threshold stream is neither
excluded nor re-scored (`unresolved_low_score_stream`), and refuses an exclusion naming a stream
nobody assessed. The commitment serialises the scores, the exclusions and the threshold, and signs
them, so the accuracy claim and the evidence for it are one record.

## Discovery sends nothing by default

Passive methods — mDNS, SSDP/WS-Discovery, ARP, DHCP observation, BACnet Who-Is, MQTT topic scan —
need no permission because they emit nothing. Active probing is opt-in, honours a do-not-probe list
that outranks the opt-in, and is rate-limited to 30 targets per minute so a sweep is not mistaken
for an attack by the customer's own monitoring.

Fingerprints map to a proposed driver with a confidence the field engineer confirms; an unmatched
fingerprint is reported to T2 as metadata to grow the point-map library. `to_t2_metadata()` carries
the service, vendor and model — not the address, and never a credential.

## Three adapters, and the fourth is refused by name

`select_adapters` refuses a fourth adapter on `software_bound` with `adapter_cap` and names the L2
upgrade path. It clamps nothing: SPEC's stated failure mode is a box that works for a week and then
OOMs on a busy Saturday, which a silent clamp would reproduce.

## Credentials stay on the customer's premises

| Refusal | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `rw_credential_forbidden_l1` | a PTZ/config-write account offered to a `software_bound` vault |
| `credential_unknown` | a credential used before it was stored |

D-004 accepts that the L1 disk is readable if the box is stolen. That makes a write-capable device
account unstorable rather than carefully stored, so `Vault.store` refuses it outright on L1 and the
same account is accepted where the vault is attested. Every use appends a `credential_use` entry
naming the credential and the purpose and never the secret, and `t2_report()` offers T2 the names
only — the evidence for "Tangri never holds your device passwords" has to be inspectable.

## Not proven here

No stream is decoded and no packet is sent. The scores are computed from measurements a probe must
take from a real camera (achieved fps in particular is a decode-path measurement, not a claim read
from the stream profile), and discovery is the policy around mDNS/SSDP listeners that do not exist
yet. The vault models the policy the TPM/SEC-1 sealed vault must enforce; it is not that vault, and
holding a secret in a Python object is not sealing it.
